About

I’m Beixi (Chloe) Du, an M.Eng. student in Financial Engineering at Cornell University, expecting to graduate in December 2026.

Since October 2026, I have been working with Yumeng He and Prof. Chenfanfu Jiang in the UCLA Artificial Intelligence and Visual Computing (AIVC) Lab, Department of Mathematics.

I study how intelligent systems learn from demonstrations, use information, and make decisions under uncertainty. My research with Prof. Kilian Q. Weinberger and Prof. Sarah Dean at Cornell spans LLM agent policies, document retrieval, and belief-space learning and control. I also work on visual reasoning evaluation through The Prestige.

Before Cornell, I earned a B.A. in Mathematics, with minors in Computer Science and Economics, from McGill University.

Shuo Wen, Beixi Du*, Edwin Meriaux*, Junming Shi*, Chloe Si*, David Meger, Doina Precup, Gregory Dudek

A video-understanding benchmark built from 180 professional magic performances and 2,177 open-ended questions. It tests temporal and causal understanding, cognitive visual reasoning, and robustness to misleading questions, revealing a substantial gap between current models and human performance.

LLM Agent Policy Recovery from Noisy Demonstrations

September 2025–October 2026

Recovered reusable decision rules from 74 noisy human-demonstration trajectories through graph-based policy extraction and statistical gap detection. In project experiments, pass@1 improved from 67.5% to 90.0%, approaching the 95.0% high-quality-demonstrator reference; pass@3 consistency increased from 42.5% to 55.0%.

Belief-Space Control for Bilinear Observation Systems

September 2025–April 2026

Studied dual control when actions affect both the system state and the quality of future observations. Implemented separation-based control, model predictive control, and belief-space MPC in PyTorch with L-BFGS, and examined how planning over uncertainty changes control cost and action selection.

Read the paper ↗

Learning Belief Representations

May–October 2026

Built a structured autoregressive estimator of belief means and covariances from action–observation histories, without state or belief supervision or training-time Kalman filtering. Studied identifiability and uncertainty modeling; achieved R2 = 0.9845 on a six-state belief-mean task.

McGill University / Cornell University · March–June 2026

Cognitive Visual Reasoning Evaluation: The Prestige

Co-developed a benchmark with 2,177 open-ended questions from 180 magic videos and evaluated 14 vision-language models against human baselines. Analyzed temporal and object-tracking failures, and found that changing the response format on the same 200 questions raised mean accuracy from 37.5% to 71.5%.

Cornell MFE Capstone · CoinbaseAugust–December 2026

Crypto Liquidation Exchange Simulator

Built a discrete-event order-book simulator with Coinbase Quantitative Risk Modeling using 193,781 SOL–USD top-of-book observations and a sampled 56.6-million-event BTC data set. Replayed 14,426 events without reconstruction inconsistencies and diagnosed rejected orders, stale snapshots, and inventory-independent quotes.

Inventory-aware quote skew improved midpoint–latent-value correlation from 0.02 to 0.49 and reduced terminal inventory from 34.5 to 2.8 units. Liquidation stress tests are ongoing.
