About

I am an M.Eng. student in Financial Engineering at Cornell University, working at the intersection of machine learning, sequential decision-making, and control.

My research explores how intelligent systems reason, learn from demonstrations, and make decisions under uncertainty. I am especially interested in reinforcement learning and robot learning, and in improving robustness, generalization, and learning efficiency in real-world settings.

Before Cornell, I earned a B.A. in Mathematics, with minors in Computer Science and Economics, from McGill University.

The Prestige: Benchmarking Cognitive Visual Reasoning using Magic Tricks

Shuo Wen, Beixi Du*, Edwin Meriaux*, Junming Shi*, Chloe Si*, David Meger, Doina Precup, Gregory Dudek

A video-understanding benchmark built from 180 professional magic performances and 2,177 open-ended questions. It tests temporal and causal understanding, cognitive visual reasoning, and robustness to misleading questions, revealing a substantial gap between current models and human performance.

Cornell ORIE · June 2026–present

Reasoning-Aware Reward Models for VLA Evaluation

Designing a human-preference-trained reward model that jointly evaluates a policy’s reasoning trace and action trajectory. The project explores label-efficient evaluation, Monte Carlo value estimates for per-step credit assignment, and statistical testing of reasoning–action faithfulness.

Cornell CS · September 2025–present

LLM Agent Policy Recovery from Noisy Demonstrations

Advisor: Prof. Kilian Q. Weinberger

A two-stage pipeline combines graph-based policy extraction with cross-trajectory statistical gap detection. In project experiments, agent pass@1 improved from 67.5% to 90.0%, against a 95.0% high-quality demonstrator ceiling; pass@3 consistency increased from 42.5% to 55.0%.

LLM agentsLearning from demonstrationsStatistical testing

Cornell CS · September 2025–April 2026

Belief-Space Control for Bilinear Observation Systems

Advisor: Prof. Sarah Dean

Implemented and benchmarked B-MPC, separation-principle, and separation-MPC controllers, using an input-dependent Kalman filter and L-BFGS optimization. Investigated how planning over uncertainty changes control cost and action selection.
